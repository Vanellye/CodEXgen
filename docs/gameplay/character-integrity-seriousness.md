# Character Integrity & Seriousness Mechanics

**Status:** Initial gameplay specification  
**Repository:** `Vanellye/CodEXgen`  
**Branch:** `cognitive-layer-2`  
**Related specification:** `docs/gameplay/debt-of-creation.md`  
**Date recorded:** 2026-11-09

## 1. Design intent

Show which characters are genuinely committed through observable behavior over time. A character's seriousness is demonstrated through consistency, fulfilled commitments, accountability, respect for consent, and conduct under pressure—not merely through dialogue, status, appearance, power, wealth, obedience, or agreement with the player.

This is a fictional gameplay model. It must not be presented as a scientifically validated measure of real human character or used to diagnose real people.

## 2. Core rule

**Characters earn credibility through evidenced actions, not declarations alone.**

The game tracks distinct dimensions rather than collapsing a character into one moral score. A character may be reliable in one area and uncertain or untested in another.

### Character dimensions

1. **Word-to-action consistency** — Does the character follow through on clear, voluntary promises?
2. **Obligation stewardship** — Does the character acknowledge, communicate, and resolve commitments they have accepted?
3. **Accountability** — Can they admit mistakes, disclose relevant facts, and make proportionate repairs?
4. **Consent and boundaries** — Do they respect another character's refusal, autonomy, and informed choice?
5. **Courage under pressure** — Do they uphold stated principles when doing so carries a meaningful, proportionate cost?
6. **Care and cooperation** — Do they contribute to shared goals without demanding control or unearned credit?
7. **Consistency over time** — Does their conduct remain reasonably aligned across different contexts, while allowing for learning and change?

## 3. Evidence model

Each observed action is recorded as an event with:

- Character ID.
- Action and narrative context.
- Related promise, obligation, or choice, if any.
- Source event or witness reference.
- Time and game chapter.
- Whether the action was voluntary, constrained, interrupted, or impossible.
- Outcome and affected parties.
- Whether the character later acknowledged or repaired the outcome.

Evidence must be contextual. A missed promise caused by imprisonment, lack of access, deception, illness in the fictional story, or another genuine constraint is not treated the same as a freely chosen betrayal. A character who renegotiates honestly before a deadline may demonstrate more integrity than one who silently abandons a promise.

The game distinguishes:
- **Observed:** directly witnessed or reliably recorded.
- **Reported:** stated by another character and not yet verified.
- **Inferred:** a possible interpretation, not established fact.
- **Contested:** credible accounts conflict.
- **Unknown:** insufficient evidence.

Do not convert rumor, popularity, charisma, combat success, or a single failure into proof of character.

## 4. Player-facing seriousness indicators

Show a readable profile with evidence-backed labels rather than an unexplained numeric morality score:

- **Follows through:** repeated commitments completed.
- **Communicates honestly:** risks and delays disclosed.
- **Makes repairs:** harm or broken commitments addressed.
- **Respects boundaries:** consent and refusal honored.
- **Untested:** not enough relevant situations have occurred.
- **Mixed record:** meaningful evidence points in different directions.
- **Under review:** evidence is disputed or incomplete.

Include short examples and dates/chapters so players can inspect why an indicator appears. Keep uncertainty visible. Characters should be able to challenge inaccurate reports and add their perspective.

## 5. Seriousness under pressure

Use authored dilemmas that reveal priorities without forcing a single approved answer. Examples:

- A character can keep a promise only by asking others to accept a new cost. Do they disclose the tradeoff and seek consent?
- A mission changes unexpectedly. Does the character communicate and renegotiate, or conceal the change?
- A character discovers their earlier belief was mistaken. Do they revise their view while preserving the record of what they previously believed?
- A powerful ally violates another character's boundary. Does the character intervene safely, seek support, or document the issue?
- Two legitimate obligations conflict. Does the character explain the conflict and negotiate a fair resolution?

Reward thoughtful communication, proportionate action, and repair—not reckless self-sacrifice, blind loyalty, aggression, or obedience.

## 6. Character growth and reversibility

Character profiles evolve through new evidence. Earlier actions remain in the history. Growth is represented by later actions and linked reflections, not by deleting inconvenient events.

A character can:
- Acknowledge a past failure.
- Offer a repair or restitution.
- Learn a different way to keep commitments.
- Dispute an inaccurate accusation with evidence.
- Change a commitment transparently when circumstances change.

Forgiveness is not mandatory for affected characters. Repair does not automatically restore trust; trust may require time and repeated evidence.

## 7. Integration with Debt of Creation

When a character commissions an in-game obligation, link it to their profile. Track whether they:

- Understood and voluntarily accepted the terms.
- Communicated when circumstances changed.
- Honored, renegotiated, or disputed the obligation.
- Repaired consequences where appropriate.

Do not treat an obligation as valid merely because it exists in a record. Preserve its origin and terms, and allow evidence-based review of coercion, misunderstanding, fraud, or invalid commission.

## 8. Safeguards

- Seriousness is not measured by willingness to fight, threaten, dominate, or suffer.
- Respectful refusal is not a character defect.
- A character is not penalized for declining an obligation they never accepted.
- No single worldview, faith, culture, personality style, or communication style is treated as the universal standard.
- Avoid gender, race, disability, class, or other demographic stereotypes in behavior modeling.
- Do not infer inner motives as facts from one outward action.
- Do not use hidden scoring to manipulate players into purchases, emotional dependence, or coercive choices.
- The system should allow multiple principled responses to the same dilemma.

## 9. Acceptance tests

1. A promise is not marked fulfilled until its defined condition is met or openly renegotiated.
2. A constrained failure is distinguishable from a voluntary breach.
3. Reported and inferred claims never display as verified observations.
4. Conflicting accounts remain visible as contested.
5. A character can challenge an inaccurate record and attach counter-evidence.
6. New growth changes current indicators without deleting historical events.
7. Respecting refusal never reduces seriousness.
8. Violence, obedience, wealth, popularity, and player agreement do not independently raise seriousness.
9. Trust restoration requires relevant new evidence and is not automatic after an apology.
10. Every displayed indicator can be traced to inspectable events.

## 10. Preservation declaration

This specification is an additive gameplay interpretation. It does not alter the original declarations, the Six Laws of Source Preservation, or the Debt of Creation source. Future revisions must be separately versioned and preserve prior evidence.

**Character principle:** Let words introduce intention; let actions provide evidence; let accountability demonstrate growth; let time reveal consistency.
