# Dimensions and Coordinate Systems

**Purpose:** Explain the mathematical space being studied and how locations, directions, and transformations are represented.

## Dimension

State what dimension means in the chosen context. Common settings include:

- Euclidean spaces such as \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\mathbb R^3\).
- Abstract vector spaces, where dimension is the size of a basis.
- Manifolds, whose dimension is locally the number of independent coordinates.
- Fractal dimension, which uses definitions such as Hausdorff or box-counting dimension and is not interchangeable with ordinary Euclidean dimension.

Do not assume every use of the word “dimension” means an additional physical spatial direction.

## Coordinates and geometry

Coordinates describe points relative to a chosen frame. The geometric object is not identical to one coordinate description; changing coordinates can represent the same object differently.

When changing frames, specify whether the transformation is a translation, rotation, reflection, scaling, affine map, or a more general coordinate transformation. State which properties are preserved.

## Projections and cross-sections

A projection or cross-section can help represent higher-dimensional mathematics in a lower-dimensional medium. Label the method and what information it preserves or discards. A 2D drawing of a 3D object and a 3D projection of a 4D object are representations, not direct views of the full higher-dimensional object.
