# Mathematical Studies

A curated set of worked studies. Each document states its mathematical setting, defines notation, presents established results or derivations, gives examples, and identifies limitations.

## Study collection

| Study | Main subject |
|---|---|
| [01 — Euclidean Geometry](EUCLIDEAN-GEOMETRY.md) | Flat metric geometry, distance, parallel postulate, Pythagorean theorem, circle geometry |
| [02 — Non-Euclidean Geometry](NON-EUCLIDEAN-GEOMETRY.md) | Spherical and hyperbolic geometry, geodesics, curvature, triangle angle excess and defect |
| [03 — Fractal Geometry](FRACTAL-GEOMETRY.md) | Iterated function systems, self-similarity, Hausdorff and similarity dimensions |
| [04 — Higher-Dimensional Geometry](HIGHER-DIMENSIONAL-GEOMETRY.md) | Euclidean n-space, hypercubes, n-dimensional distance, volume, projections |

## Reading path

1. Begin with Euclidean geometry to establish distance, angle, and proof conventions.
2. Compare spherical and hyperbolic geometries to see how curvature changes familiar results.
3. Study fractals to examine dimensions that need not be integers.
4. Study higher-dimensional geometry to generalize coordinates and familiar solids to arbitrary finite dimension.

## Standards

- All formulae state their geometric setting and relevant assumptions.
- Proofs and derivations are distinguished from examples and numerical checks.
- A diagram is an aid to reasoning, not a substitute for proof.
- Conjectures and open problems are labeled; established theorems are not presented as unresolved.
- Physical interpretations are not inferred from mathematical models without separate evidence.
