# Axioms, Theorems, Lemmas, and Proofs

**Purpose:** Make the logical structure of geometric knowledge explicit.

## Logical roles

- **Axiom / postulate:** an assumption adopted as a starting point within a specified formal system.
- **Theorem:** a proposition established by proof from definitions, axioms, and previously proved results.
- **Lemma:** an auxiliary proved proposition used to establish another result.
- **Corollary:** a result that follows directly from a theorem or established proposition.
- **Proposition:** a mathematical statement proved to be true; usage varies by discipline.

An axiom is not a theorem. A conjecture remains unproved until a valid proof is supplied.

## Proof quality checklist

1. State all assumptions and the exact conclusion.
2. Identify the formal system (for example, Euclidean or hyperbolic geometry).
3. Define every symbol and object.
4. Justify each inference; do not skip a step that depends on a non-obvious result.
5. Avoid circular reasoning and hidden assumptions.
6. Check degenerate cases and boundary conditions.
7. Separate a proof from a numerical check, diagram, analogy, or simulation.
8. If a proof relies on a named result, cite it and state the version being used.

## Geometry-specific caution

A diagram can suggest a result but is not, by itself, a proof. Visual appearance may hide exceptional cases, such as coincident points, zero-length segments, parallel lines, or degenerate polygons.
