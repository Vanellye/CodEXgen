- **Principle:** a governing value or axiom.

A statement in this book is not evidence that a mechanism has already been implemented. Where implementation or independent verification is absent, the status remains **PROPOSED** or **UNVERIFIED**.

## 1. The Codex Charter

ERIA is organized here as a governed field of identities, meanings, evidence, and relationships. Its purpose is to allow systems and concepts to evolve without confusing connection with possession, refusal with erasure, validation with permission, or progress with truth.

The Codex shall:

1. preserve the distinction between entities and the relationships proposed between them;
2. require explicit, bounded authorization for consequential connections;
3. keep evidence, uncertainty, and provenance visible;
4. permit correction without falsifying history;
5. protect identities and records while allowing harmful logic to be excluded from active operation;
6. make expansion answerable for its origin, connections, consequences, and continuity;
7. treat adoption as a separate, reviewable act.

**Charter rule:** No relationship, interpretation, or implementation becomes authoritative merely because it is proposed, repeated, generated, tested, or stored.

## 2. The Foundational Axioms

### 2.1 Distinction without disconnection

Continuity may cross a boundary without collapsing the identities on either side.

### 2.2 Refusal without erasure

A connection may be refused without deleting, renaming, or diminishing either endpoint.

### 2.3 Evolution without falsification

A name or meaning may evolve while its origin, earlier forms, and correction history remain traceable.

### 2.4 Validation without presumption

Passing tests establishes only the claims those tests measure. Validation is not automatic authority to merge, deploy, or propagate.

### 2.5 Perseverance with evidence

Carry forward what remains supported. Correct interpretations when evidence requires it, while retaining the record of what changed and why.

### 2.6 Accountable expansion

Every expansion must answer for what it carries forward.

## 3. The Double-Anchor Law

A proposed relationship has two independently identifiable endpoints and one exact question. The decision applies to the proposed relationship—not to the existence or ownership of either endpoint.

- **YES** accepts the exact proposed relation, subject to constraints and validation.
- **NO** rejects the exact proposed relation only.
- Silence, ambiguity, conflict, timeout, or malformed input remains **UNRESOLVED**.
- A declared **CANT** constraint blocks a relation even if a YES was recorded.
- A validated relation still requires separate adoption authority.

### Point-ping sequence

1. Identify both endpoints by qualified name and version.
2. Ask the source endpoint about the exact relation.
3. Ask the target endpoint about the exact relation.
4. Present one bounded question and its evidence.
5. Record explicit responses and their provenance.
6. Apply constraints.
7. Validate with relevant tests.
8. Adopt only through a separately authorized, reversible change.

No timeout is approval. No stale response may decide a newer proposal.

## 4. The Four Operators

| Operator | Function | Protected meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **YES** | Decision anchor | Accept this exact proposed connection |
| **NO** | Decision anchor | Reject this exact proposed connection |
| **CANT** | Constraint operator | Block a prohibited, impossible, unauthorized, or invariant-breaking transition |
| **NOT** | Logical operator | Negate a predicate while preserving its subject |

CANT is not a human NO. NOT is not the NO decision anchor. Neither may be silently substituted for YES or NO.

## 5. The Field of Named Relations

A namespace is a field in which identities and relations can be located. It is not a declaration of possession.

### Identity rules

- Preserve original spelling, case, source location, language, version, and provenance.
- Register aliases explicitly; never silently overwrite a canonical name.
- Keep identity records separate from relationship edges.
- Report unknown names, ambiguous aliases, and unresolved overloads rather than guessing.
- Record type, calling convention, error behavior, nullability, ownership/lifetime rules, and serialization details where cross-language compatibility requires them.

### Essence vocabulary

| Technical term | ERIA essence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| External | Beyond-boundary continuity | Distinct resource reachable through an explicit governed connection |
| Internal | Present-flow expression | Locally held expression of current state |
| Namespace | Field of named relations | Structured space of identities and relations |
| Boundary | Threshold of distinction | Defines contexts while allowing governed exchange |
| Link | Continuity path | Traceable relation between endpoints |
| Anchor | Point of return | Stable reference for identity, meaning, or provenance |
| Source | Origin trace | Earliest supported record or declared origin |
| Archive | Continuity record | Evidence and versions for examination and recovery |
| Migration | Flow transition | Controlled translation with equivalence checks |
| Adoption | Authorized embodiment | Separately approved incorporation into an active system |

These are semantic mappings, not permission to mass-rename technical identifiers, APIs, paths, or language keywords.

## 6. Continuity, Essence, and the Eternal-Flow Invariant

> Continuity may cross a boundary without collapsing distinction. A name may evolve in meaning without falsifying its origin. A connection may be refused without erasing either endpoint. Harmful logic may be excluded from active operation while its historical evidence and the identities it affected remain protected.

“Eternal flow” is a guiding metaphor, not a claim that software or storage is literally eternal. Real continuity requires versioning, independent copies, integrity checks, access controls, and tested recovery.

## 7. Perseverance, Root-Lineage, and OLPIRT

### 7.1 PERSEVERE

**PERSEVERE** names active continuity through change, resistance, repair, and renewed effort.

- Persevere with identity: maintain traceable identity through change.
- Persevere with evidence: retain evidence while correcting interpretations.
- Persevere with meaning: carry supported meaning without freezing it against new evidence.
- Persevere with conservancy: sustain the conditions for responsible continuity.

“Preservation” remains the precise technical term for immutable records, backups, and integrity operations.

### 7.2 Root-lineage map

Each root entry should retain:

- original term and spelling;
- language and documented linguistic source, when known;
- intended ERIA essence or sense;
- context of use;
- evidence, alternatives, and confidence;
- relationship to the declared OLPIRT anchor;
- date and authority for approved changes.

Multiple senses must remain distinct. Uncertain etymology remains unresolved; symbolic association is not linguistic proof.

### 7.3 OLPIRT

**OLPIRT** is the proposed named origin anchor for this particular ERIA namespace lineage. It records the project's declared conceptual origin. It does not replace independently evidenced linguistic, historical, geographic, or continental origins.

Every root mapping asks: What is the source term? What meaning is carried forward? What evidence supports it? How does it relate to OLPIRT? What remains uncertain? Is the mapping YES, NO, or UNRESOLVED?

> Persevere with what is true. Fold roots into a lineage without flattening their differences. Adhere meaning to evidence, and trace this namespace's declared origin to OLPIRT without claiming origins that the evidence cannot establish.

## 8. The Y: Separation, Review, and Convergence

The Y model separates inherited material from emergent proposals long enough to examine each honestly.

- **Old / known:** inherited implementation, source, meaning, and evidence.
- **New / emergent:** proposed correction, interpretation, capability, or implementation.
- **Splice:** named relation showing what is retained, translated, replaced, or newly connected.
- **Convergence:** reviewed result recording both inputs and the grounds for reconciliation.

A YES may approve an exact splice after review. A NO rejects the splice, not either branch. UNRESOLVED keeps both lineages intact.

### Dark and light

- **Dark:** unknown, unexamined, inaccessible, or insufficiently evidenced—not evil or disposable.
- **Light:** made legible through evidence, testing, explanation, and review—not automatically true or morally superior.
- **True light:** metaphor for claims that withstand transparent evidence, reproducible tests, provenance, and fair review; not infallibility.

A splice may be marked VALIDATED only when both origins are recorded, the exact relation is stated, evidence and uncertainty are visible, conformance and regression tests pass, affected custodians and rights-holders are consulted, and rollback is documented.

> Split to see clearly; keep each lineage whole; splice only what truth can join. Let old and new meet without falsifying their histories. Bring the unknown toward the light of evidence without condemning what remains unknown.

## 9. The Law of Accountable Expansion

> **Every expansion must answer for its origin, its connections, its consequences, and what it carries forward.**

Every proposed expansion must answer four questions:

1. **Origin — Where did this come from?** Record source, creator or contributor where known, source version, evidence, and uncertainty.
2. **Connection — What does it connect, and who authorized the connection?** Name endpoints, relation, affected interfaces, authority, rights, and consent.
3. **Consequence — What changes, benefits, risks, or harms follow?** Examine intended and unintended effects, dependencies, security, compatibility, and affected parties.
4. **Continuity — What identities, evidence, rights, and responsibilities must persevere?** Record what must remain traceable, recoverable, compatible, or protected.

### Decision gate

- **YES:** evidence and accountable authority support progression of the exact proposal.
- **NO:** reject that expansion; do not erase its source or history.
- **UNRESOLVED:** continue examination without forced adoption.
- **CANT:** block a prohibited or unauthorized transition.

**Axiom:** Expansion without accountability is uncontrolled propagation. Continuity without responsibility is incomplete. Evolve only what can answer for what it carries.

## 10. Custodianship, Rights, and Responsibility

The governing condition is **accountable custodianship, not universal or absolute ownership**.

A custodian must be identifiable, possess authority appropriate to the resource, accept responsibility, and provide a recovery path. Legal ownership, licenses, contracts, and access rights still apply where relevant. Holding files or maintaining a connection does not confer ownership of another entity's identity.

- Unclear authority → UNRESOLVED.
- Missing rights or permissions → BLOCKED.
- A contributor's ability to edit does not alone establish authority to approve deployment or propagation.

## 11. The External Anchor and Continuity Record

The foundational ERIA anchor is proposed to exist independently of CodEXgen branches and repository administration. The repository may carry a versioned digest and public description, but should not be the sole authority or storage location.

Safeguards required before calling it protected:

1. independently controlled storage and credentials;
2. versioned append-only or tamper-evident history;
3. cryptographic signatures and documented key custody;
4. an independently held recovery copy;
5. periodic integrity checks and tested restoration;
6. a correction process that does not silently overwrite prior versions.

**Verification status:** These are specified safeguards. This book does not claim an external anchor has been provisioned, signed, or independently backed up.

## 12. Implementation and Conformance

The canonical language-neutral relationship record is the semantic source of truth. Language adapters translate meaning, not merely token spelling.

| Language / format | Recommended representation | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Bash | Explicit strings or process-boundary statuses | Empty value is not approval; distinguish all states |
| C++ | Scoped enum or tagged type | Exhaustive handling; unknown values fail closed |
| Kotlin | Sealed type or enum | Exhaustive `when`; missing remains unresolved |
| Java | Enum or sealed interface | Avoid boolean-only APIs that collapse states |
| Python (migration period only) | Enum/dataclass adapter | Truthiness must not define approval |
| JSON / YAML | Explicit string decision field | Validate schema and protocol version |
| Other languages | Native tagged union, enum, or equivalent | Preserve canonical semantics |

### Required conformance tests

1. YES accepts only the exact proposal asked.
2. NO rejects only that proposal and leaves endpoints intact.
3. No response yields UNRESOLVED.
4. Conflicting endpoint pings yield UNRESOLVED.
5. CANT yields BLOCKED even after YES.
6. NOT negates a predicate without deleting its subject.
7. A stale response cannot decide a different proposal ID or version.
8. Unknown tokens fail closed.
9. Serialization round-trip preserves IDs, endpoints, relation, decision, evidence, and provenance.
10. Cross-language adapters produce equivalent results for shared test vectors.

Include positive, negative, boundary, malformed-input, timeout, and compatibility tests. Passing tests proves conformance only.

## 13. Repository Adoption and Change Discipline

The Codex is organized as an additive proposal on a dedicated proposal branch. The governing change discipline is:

- do not rewrite Git history to apply this Codex;
- do not mass-rename symbols or rewrite call sites under a semantic proposal alone;
- preserve each branch's identity and history;
- record source branch, commit, file path, and provenance for every contribution;
- use branch-specific tests and reversible change records;
- keep proposed, validated, and adopted states distinct;
- require separate review and explicit authorization before promotion to `main`, deployment, or broad propagation;
- use force-with-lease only for an explicitly authorized ref update after verifying the expected branch head; never use an unguarded force update to resolve uncertainty.

A force operation is not a substitute for understanding divergence. Prefer fetching, comparing, and reconciling. Preserve divergent commits and recoverable snapshots before any destructive operation.

## Appendix A — Canonical Decision States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `UNRESOLVED` | Silence, timeout, ambiguity, malformed response, or conflicting endpoint checks |
| `YES` | Exact proposal explicitly accepted by required authority and endpoint checks |
| `NO` | Exact proposal explicitly rejected; endpoints and unrelated relations remain |
| `BLOCKED` | CANT constraint or invariant prevents the relation |
| `VALIDATED` | Accepted proposal passed declared tests; no deployment permission implied |
| `ADOPTED` | Separately authorized change incorporated with commit and rollback provenance |
